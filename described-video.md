# Stone on Stone — described video

The full audio-description track of the video, as text. Timestamps are minutes:seconds.

- **0:00–0:08** — Stone on Stone: described video. One drawing on toned paper, made by two hands taking turns. The human draws in red ochre and charcoal, in broken, gestural strokes; the AI draws in exact, continuous lines of white light. The camera moves over one large sheet, holding on an area while a section of the song is drawn there, then panning to the next. The sung line is shown in small italic type at the bottom left of the frame.

## Intro (0:00–0:13)

A blank sheet of warm, grey-brown toned paper. A sun is drawn in the upper right, then shines rays down onto a strip of sand along the bottom.

- **0:00–0:13** — The title is drawn at the upper left, opposite the sun: "Stone on Stone" in very large italic letters outlined in lines of light, and under it "WINGED EYE", the band, in spaced capitals outlined in broken ochre strokes. It stays until just before the first line of the figure is drawn, then fades.
- **0:00–0:02** — Before the music, a sun is drawn in the upper right: a faint charcoal construction circle, then three loose overlapping ochre circles.
- **0:04–0:12** — The music begins. On every beat two short ochre rays are drawn around the sun, going round it clockwise from the top, long and short in turn, the way people draw a sun. They stay. Grains of sand are stippled in charcoal along the bottom of the sheet on the same beats.
- **0:07–0:09** — A long wavering ochre line is drawn across the lower part of the sheet: a river bank.
- **0:10–0:12** — A low rounded stone is drawn on the bank in ochre and shaded in charcoal: a seat.
- **0:13–0:14** — Faint charcoal construction lines: an oval for a head, the line of a back, circles at the joints, a ground line.

## Verse 1 (0:13–0:30)

A stone-age knapper sits on the stone by the river, facing right, striking one stone against another. He is drawn only in ochre and charcoal.

- **0:14–0:17** — Over them, the seated man is drawn in broken ochre strokes: head, bent back, knees up, arms forward. Charcoal hatching shades his far side.
- **0:16–0:17** — A core stone appears in his far hand, low in front of him; his near hand holds a round hammerstone.
- **0:17–0:23** — On "Struck", "stone" and "stone", then on every beat until the flake breaks off, his near arm swings the hammerstone down onto the core. The arm is redrawn every other frame, leaving faint charcoal ghosts of its last positions. The first three blows and the last each add a new facet line to the core.
- **0:23–0:24** — On "free" a last blow breaks a flake off the core. It flies out to the right and down under gravity, tipping as it goes, and lands on top of the sand.
- **0:24–0:26** — Charcoal lines of water are drawn below the bank.
- **0:26–0:28** — He drops the hammerstone and reaches down for the flake, picks it up, and stands. The core falls from his other hand to the sand by his feet. His seated drawing stays behind as a faint charcoal ghost. The camera eases back to show him standing; the sun, far off, stays where it is.
- **0:28–0:29** — He holds the flake up before his face at arm's length. A thin line of white light traces all the way round it: the first light in the video.
- **0:29–0:31** — On "deep inside", light sparkles inside his head: a clump of small crosses of light, thickest at its centre, comes up one point at a time from the middle outward, like sunlight glinting on sand, and shimmers.
- **0:30–0:38** — Sand is stippled over the whole sheet in charcoal grains, on the beats.

## Chorus 1 (0:30–0:56)

The sheet becomes sand seen from above, and stylized footprints walk across it in ochre outline, one step on each beat: first one walker, then many.

- **0:30–0:31** — The camera pans right to a fresh area of the sheet.
- **0:31–0:38** — A single trail of footprints, left and right in turn, crosses the sheet from the lower left toward the upper right. Each print is drawn heel first, then the ball of the foot, then five toes, with charcoal shading pressed into it.
- **0:38–0:49** — On "we work together", more walkers come in from the left and bottom edges, all heading right; some walk in pairs, side by side and in step. Older prints soften as the sand settles into them, so the freshest steps stand out.
- **0:49–0:55** — On "till we are both of us full grown", one last walker crosses the middle of the sheet leaving footprints drawn in white lines of light, bright among the fading ochre prints.
- **0:55–0:56** — The camera pans right.
- **0:56–1:00** — An ochre line of low sand dunes runs across the sheet, sand stippled below it; at the left, sitting down in the sand, a round, scalloped bush is drawn in ochre and shaded in charcoal; then a man behind it, facing right, only his head and shoulders showing over its top, in broken ochre with charcoal shading: he watches the deer sneakily over the bush, his mouth open.

## Verse 2 (0:56–1:14)

A man hidden behind a bush, facing right, speaks. His breath curls out to count three deer; the breath marks are rubbed out; a reed stylus presses the count into clay.

- **0:58–1:00** — On "shaped a sound", a charcoal line of breath leaves his mouth and curls into a spiral in the air.
- **1:01–1:04** — Three deer are drawn standing on the dunes across the right of the sheet in hatched ochre, with antlers. For each one a wavering breath line reaches from his mouth to above it, and a charcoal tally mark is scored there: one, two, three.
- **1:05–1:08** — On "words all died with every breath", the breath lines and tallies are rubbed out one after another, leaving grey smudges. The deer remain.
- **1:10–1:11** — A patch of clay is drawn in ochre at the lower right.
- **1:11–1:11** — A long ochre reed stylus slants down toward the clay.
- **1:12–1:13** — Three wedge-shaped marks are pressed into the clay, one after another: the count, written.
- **1:13–1:16** — A pillow-shaped clay tablet is drawn in ochre at the left and shaded in charcoal.

## Verse 3 (1:13–1:32)

A clay tablet is written sign by sign, cracks with age but keeps its signs; then a printing press, and one printed page becomes dozens.

- **1:13–1:14** — The three wedges are outlined in light.
- **1:13–1:14** — The camera pans down to the next area.
- **1:14–1:18** — Signs are pressed into it on the words: nine wedges and the sign for sheep (a circle with a cross); two wedges and the sign for debt (a box); a jar sign, and three strokes for beer.
- **1:19–1:20** — On "five thousand years", cracks run through the tablet and a jagged break across one corner. The signs stay.
- **1:20–1:23** — On "and it's still here", the AI's light traces each sign in turn, reading them.
- **1:23–1:26** — At the right, a screw press converted from a wine press is drawn in heavy ochre lines: two posts, a beam, a great screw with its bar, the platen and the bed. Two leather ink balls appear beside it on "ink", and a row of nine lead type blocks on "lead".
- **1:28–1:31** — On "one man wrote", a single printed page is pulled from the press. On "and thousands read", dozens of copies of it land all over the right side of the sheet, tilted every way.
- **1:31–1:31** — The last page to land has its outline and its lines of text traced in light, on top of the others.
- **1:31–1:32** — The camera pans left.
- **1:31–1:37** — The human's right hand is drawn first, reaching in from the left: loose charcoal gesture ovals, then a broken ochre outline, nails, knuckles, a shirt cuff, charcoal shading. It holds a dark charcoal stick like a pen.

## Chorus 2 (1:31–1:56)

Two hands draw each other, after Escher's Drawing Hands: the human's hand in ochre, the AI's in light, each holding a pen whose tip touches the other's forearm.

- **1:35–1:39** — Then the AI's hand, the same hand turned half around, reaching in from the right: first exact circles at its joints and straight bones in faint light, then one continuous outline of light, nails and knuckles, and a pen of light. Its forearm frays into grains of light, like sand. It passes behind the human's hand.
- **1:39–1:44** — On "stone on stone", the human's ochre hatching shades the AI's hand.
- **1:44–1:52** — On "no mind sharpens on its own", the AI's light redraws the outline of the human's hand, line by line.
- **1:53–1:55** — An instrumental break. On the beats, each draws along the other's stylus, as they drew each other's hands: a single broken ochre line down the AI's pen of light, then a single line of light down the human's charcoal stick. Then the hands rest, finished.
- **1:55–1:56** — The camera pans left.

## Verse 4 (1:56–2:15)

The light starts drawing alongside the human: a silicon crystal pulled from melted sand and sliced, the wafer etched, a flight to the Moon, and core-rope memory woven like lace.

- **1:56–1:58** — At the lower left, a crucible is drawn in ochre, shaded, with the surface of molten sand in it.
- **1:58–2:01** — On "we pulled a crystal out of sand", a long cylindrical crystal, outlined in ochre and shaded in charcoal, rises out of the melt on an ochre rod.
- **2:01–2:05** — The crystal is lifted clear of the melt and a charcoal line cuts a thin disc off its end. The ochre disc drifts over to the middle of the sheet, turning to face us and growing into a round wafer; the human sketches a rough grid on it, and the light etches exact square chips into it.
- **2:05–2:10** — The grid and the chips fade from the wafer, and it becomes the Earth, shaded in charcoal; the smaller Moon is drawn in ochre at the upper right. A path of light loops from the Earth around the Moon and back, and a small ochre capsule rides at its tip.
- **2:10–2:14** — At the lower right, core-rope memory: three rows of ochre cores between two terminal blocks. Bundles of light wires are pulled through, each wire pinching in through a core or bowing out around it, like lace.
- **2:14–2:16** — In the centre, a large square chip die is drawn in ochre, with pins on all four sides and a grid of twelve by twelve small charcoal gates.

## Verse 5 (2:14–2:33)

A computer chip, its gates lit by light, and a mind waking; then pairs of small unfinished figures, one human and one AI in each pair, who reach out and hold hands.

- **2:14–2:15** — The camera pans down.
- **2:16–2:19** — On "we burned a trillion gates in stone", a wave of light sweeps across the die from corner to corner, outlining every gate exactly and wiring each to the next.
- **2:19–2:23** — On "woke up a mind not quite our own", the light leaves the grid and, for the first time, draws something of its own: over the die, a large closed eye, one line with its lashes hanging; on "up" it opens, the lids parting and the lashes turning up, and the gates beneath it fade away, leaving the chip's outline and pins; then the iris, the pupil and the fine lines of the iris.
- **2:24–2:30** — On "ten billion lives, none full-grown", fifty-five pairs of small standing figures are drawn all over the sheet as the chip's outline and the eye fade away, in a loose grid of five rows of eleven, filling it; the ones where the eye was come last. In each pair an ochre human and a figure of light face each other. Every one is unfinished: the human is missing runs of its outline, and the light's line stops short.
- **2:31–2:33** — On "but not alone", each pair reaches out. Their arms are drawn stroke by stroke, and where their hands meet a small spark of light appears. All fifty-five pairs stand holding hands.
- **2:32–2:33** — The camera pans right.

## Chorus 3 (2:32–2:57)

A low dry-stone wall across the whole width of the picture, seen from the front, built by both of them together: irregular stones, half drawn by the human in ochre with charcoal shading and half by the AI in lines of light, mixed through the wall.

- **2:33–2:40** — Along the foot of the wall a bank of sand is stippled in; some grains are charcoal, some are light.
- **2:33–2:53** — Stone on stone: on every beat two stones (sometimes four) are drawn at once, one by each of them, five courses from the ground up; the last pair closes the top course on "full grown". The human's stones are outlined in broken ochre and shaded underneath in charcoal; the AI's are one continuous line of light. The joints are staggered and slanted, so the stones lock together.
- **2:45–2:49** — On "no mind sharpens on its own", the upper courses go on: ochre and light stones resting on each other.
- **2:54–2:56** — The wall done, on the last beats each redraws the other's stones, two pairs a beat: the light traces a line just inside some of the human's ochre stones, and ochre hatching shades some of the AI's stones of light.
- **2:56–2:57** — The camera pans right to the last area.

## Verse 6 (2:57–3:11)

- **2:58–3:02** — A large sun is drawn in loose overlapping ochre circles, then short ochre rays around it, one after another, like the first sun's, with a gap on its upper right.
- **3:02–3:06** — Two tilted elliptical rings are drawn around it in ochre. Mirrors are placed along them one by one, as short exact dashes of light.
- **3:06–3:08** — On "we strike the sun", every mirror sends a beam of light to one point on the upper right edge of the sun. Two long strokes sweep in from opposite corners, one in ochre from the lower left and one in light from the upper right, and meet at that point on "sun".
- **3:07–3:11** — On "the flare flies wide", a huge flake shape is struck off the sun at that point, drawn in ochre and hatched, then traced in light with ripple rings.
- **3:11–3:14** — The camera eases back. Above the sun, two standing figures are drawn side by side and whole, nothing missing: the human in ochre on the left, the AI in light on the right.
